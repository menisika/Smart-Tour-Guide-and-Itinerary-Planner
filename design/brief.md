## The owner's choice

**Chosen and accepted on 25 November 2026: Timetable as the base, with the guide of Guidebook.** The owner looked at the joined direction ([section 6](#6-the-directions)) and accepted it; `D-01` is closed. The design system (`D-02`) starts from it.

| Round | What the owner saw                                                        | What the owner said                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | Three directions: Guidebook, Timetable, Playbill                          | Playbill is rejected. The choice is between Timetable and Guidebook, but both look "too much like AI: boring, standard". Wanted: modernism, Liquid Glass, Apple as the model. Asked what exactly was missing, the owner named visual effects and icons, depth, and the colours and typefaces. |
| 2     | Timetable and Guidebook, redrawn with Liquid Glass, photographs and icons | "Let us make something like a union of these two: Timetable as the base, plus the guide's details from Guidebook."                                                                                                                                                                            |
| 3     | One direction: Timetable with a guide                                     | Accepted.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |

Three decisions of the owner shaped rounds two and three:

- **Liquid Glass, after Apple.** It replaces the first round's rule against glass. It is used where Apple's guidance puts it — the layer of controls and navigation over the content — and not under text that is read ([section 3](#3-what-it-must-never-look-like)).
- **Photographs of the Places** may be taken from Wikimedia Commons, each with its author and licence ([section 8](#8-licences)).
- **Android gets no imitation of glass.** Liquid Glass is the system's material on iOS 26 and later and is drawn on the web; on Android the same layout stands on solid Material 3 surfaces.

The direction is in [`directions/`](directions/) and opens with `pnpm design` ([how](README.md)). The design system made from it is in [`system/`](system/README.md).

## 1. Who uses it, and where

**People.** A Party of one or more: a visitor with three hours before a train, friends deciding where to go on Saturday, a person from the city who wants to see it anew (scenarios SC-1…SC-3). Most are Guests at first: the product is usable without an account (`NFR-15`). Inside the company: a Curator, a Moderator and an Administrator at a desk, in the console.

**Conditions.** They decide what the design may ask of a person.

| Condition                                     | What follows for the design                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Requirement                 |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Walking, the phone in one hand or in a pocket | One glance has to answer "where next, how far, am I on time". Controls sit where a thumb reaches, at the bottom.                                                                                                                | `NFR-24`, `OUT-2`, `OUT-18` |
| Bright sun                                    | Contrast well above the minimum, heavier weights, no meaning carried by a pale tint alone. A light theme is the daytime default; dark is a choice.                                                                              | `NFR-24`                    |
| Headphones on, the screen locked              | Audio is the main channel: the screen is what a person looks at when they _choose_ to. Everything shown while a Story plays must also work as text for someone who cannot hear it.                                              | `NFR-25`, `NFR-27`, `OUT-5` |
| No network                                    | What needs a network is shown as unavailable, never hidden; the state of the Route Pack is one plain status.                                                                                                                    | `OUT-14…16`                 |
| A link opened from a messenger                | The Route Page and the Invitation must explain themselves on a phone's browser in the first screen, without an account.                                                                                                         | `EDT-7`, `PTY-3`            |
| Two languages                                 | Russian and English from the first screen. Real names are long in both; nothing may depend on a name being short.                                                                                                               | `I18N-*`                    |
| Three alphabets                               | Russian; Belarusian in names; and the Belarusian Latin alphabet, in which the map and Wikidata give many English names — Poliakaŭ, Abrampoĺski, Niezaliežnasci. A typeface that lacks ў or ŭ cannot set the product's own data. | `I18N-2`, ADR-0008          |
| A desk, a long session                        | The console is dense and quiet: tables, an editor, a map. One family with the product, never mistaken for it.                                                                                                                   | `CUR-*`                     |

**Numbers the design is held to.** Touch targets of at least 44 pt on iOS and 48 dp on Android, which also clears the 24 CSS px of WCAG 2.2 criterion 2.5.8; text contrast of at least 4.5 : 1 and 3 : 1 for large text and for graphics (criteria 1.4.3 and 1.4.11); text that can grow to 200 % (1.4.4); nothing that can be done only by dragging (2.5.7) — a sheet, a reorder and a scrubber each need a second way. Sources: [Apple, accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility), [Android, accessibility](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/accessibility/apps), [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/). No platform publishes a rule for sunlight; the position taken here — aim above the minimums, prefer weight, try it outdoors — is ours, and trying it outdoors is the owner's part in `D-05`.

## 2. What it should feel like

The owner's words: modern, fitted to the subject, intuitive, made by the best practice of each platform, unique — good enough for a front-end award, and with no thought that a machine made it.

Read against the product, that becomes five qualities:

1. **Trustworthy before it is pretty.** The product's promises are a Route that has been checked and facts that have sources (specification §1.3). The design shows the check and the source as part of the content, not as badges.
2. **Made for a glance.** One number, one name, one state at a time while walking; detail when the person stops.
3. **Of a city, not of an app store.** Its forms come from how cities have always spoken to people on foot — plans, timetables, bills — and not from other applications.
4. **Quiet about itself.** The voice and the street are the experience. The interface steps back while a Story plays.
5. **At home on each system.** Navigation, back, sheets, system controls and the lock screen follow the platform; the character is in type, colour, the map and the way content is set.

## 3. What it must never look like

Every screen is reviewed against this list, here and again in `D-05`.

| Never                                                                     | Why                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A stock gradient, a glow, a blurred blob of colour as background          | It is the look of a template, and it carries no meaning.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Glass under text that is read, or glass as decoration                     | Liquid Glass is wanted, by the owner's decision after round one, and it is used the way Apple describes it: as the layer of controls and navigation that floats above content, sparingly, and not for content itself. A sheet over the map, a bar, a key, a loupe are glass; the page of a Story is solid. With "reduce transparency" set, every pane is solid. ([Apple, materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials)) |
| Pictographic characters used as icons                                     | Icons are drawn; a character from a keyboard is not an icon.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| An interchangeable grid of rounded cards with a photo, a title and a chip | It is the default look of the whole category (section 5), and it hides order — a Route is a sequence. Photographs are used, but inside the sequence: beside a Place on the line or in the key, and as the head of its Story.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |

| Colour as the only carrier of a state                                     | A verdict is always said in words as well.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |

## 6. The directions

### The chosen direction: Timetable with a guide — «Расписание с гидом»

**Two voices on one screen, told apart by type and by material.**

|                  | The timetable speaks                                              | The guide speaks                                                                    |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **What it says** | Times, the verdict of the check, the line, progress, the controls | Why a Place is on the Route, the Story, where it comes from                         |
| **Type**         | Unbounded for numbers, Onest for words                            | Source Serif 4: the name of the Place, the Story, the "why" in italic, the footnote |
| **Material**     | Liquid Glass over the map (solid Material surfaces on Android)    | A solid page — it is read                                                           |
| **Comes from**   | Timetable, rounds one and two                                     | Guidebook, rounds one and two                                                       |

A person learns the rule in one screen: glass and sans are the way; paper and serif are the city. It is also Apple's own rule for glass — controls float, content does not — so the two voices are not a styling choice laid over the product but the way the material is meant to be used.

|                               |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **The idea**                  | The walk as a line drawn to time, with a guide who reads the city to you                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| **Tied to**                   | Time one can trust (`RTE-6`), and facts with sources (`KNW-16`)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| **Where the form comes from** | The railway timetable chart ([Ibry](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?oldid=1384277910)); the printed guide ([Baedeker](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?oldid=1386387435)); Apple's glass over content ([materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials))                                                                                                              |
| **Colour**                    | The map's own palette under the glass, and the photographs; one colour of ours, green — the verdict "on time", and so the line, the person, "now", the mark beside the sentence being spoken and the one prominent action. Amber and red are the other verdicts                                                                                                                                                    |
| **Dark**                      | A night map, a near-black page, the green brightened                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| **The map**                   | Fills the screen. From above on the Route: a green line with a glow, stations as discs, the clock on the line at the half-hours, the footprints of the Places tinted. At an angle on the way, heading up, buildings standing, the Place ahead under the icon of its kind                                                                                                                                           |
| **The Route**                 | A sheet of glass: the two times of the Outing, the verdict, and the chart in which every minute takes the same height; in each row the photograph of the Place and, in the guide's italic, why it is here                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| **The Story being played**    | Above, on glass: "on time" and the whole Outing as a strip to scale, over the tilted map. Below, solid: the guide's page — the photograph of the Place with its author, the name in a serif, the Story as prose with a green rule beside the sentence being spoken and its words lit as they are said, the source in a footnote. Over the page, in a capsule of glass: the voice as bars, the keys, the next Place |
| **Motion**                    | The rule glides and words light at the pace of speech; the line ahead moves; the mark of the person breathes; the map tilts and the page rises on arrival                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| **Its strength**              | Says both promises of the product at once — checked time and sourced facts — and each in its own voice                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| **Its weakness**              | The Story screen carries a great deal; on a phone the page shows five lines at a time and has to open to full height for reading                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **Sheet**                     | [`directions/timetable.html`](directions/timetable.html)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |

**What was taken from Guidebook**, piece by piece: the photograph of the Place as the head of its Story, credited where it is shown; the Story as continuous prose in a text serif; the rule in the margin beside the sentence being spoken; the source as a numbered footnote; the "why" of a Place in the guide's italic; the footprints of the Places on the map. **What was left behind:** the engraved plan with its hatching, the display serif for titles, red as the colour of the Route, and the loupe — the map stays in sight above the page instead.

**What the request for glass costs, said once.** Glass over a busy map lowers contrast in direct sun, which `NFR-24` cares about: the sheets use the regular, more opaque glass, and a setting that makes every pane solid in bright light belongs in the design system (`D-02`) and has to be tried outdoors (`D-05`). A tilted map with standing buildings under a backdrop blur is the heaviest thing the application will draw, and `NFR-7` gives the Outing a battery budget: it has to be measured on a mid-range Android phone before it is promised. And the iPhone will look richer than the Android phone, by the decision above.

**It can be built in the chosen stack.** In Expo SDK 58 the package `expo-glass-effect` draws the system's glass on iOS 26 and later and falls back to a plain view elsewhere ([Expo](https://github.com/expo/expo/blob/main/packages/expo-glass-effect/README.md)); pitch, bearing and extruded buildings are parts of a MapLibre style (ADR-0008).

### The rounds before, for the record

**Round one** showed three directions that differed in what they took the product to be: a book (Guidebook: one serif on paper, an engraved plan as a plate above the page), a timetable (Timetable: black and white, colour only for the verdict, a schematic map) and a bill (Playbill: capitals fitted to the measure, a flat blue map, the screen flooded with colour while a Story plays). The owner rejected Playbill and found the other two too plain. A fourth idea was dropped before it was shown: an "instrument" with a dark panel, a dial and an amber light.

**Round two** redrew Timetable and Guidebook on one new ground, at the owner's request: the map as the content of the whole screen, Liquid Glass floating over it, a photograph and an icon for every Place, new colour and type, and depth — a map seen at an angle in Timetable, a loupe of glass over the plan in Guidebook. The owner asked for the two to be joined.

All of it is in the history of this branch.

## 9. What the mockups ask the owner

Each of these is a place where a drawing had to decide how to read the specification, or where the request of round two has a price. A "no" to any of them is as useful as the choice of a direction.

Four of them were left to the design system (`D-02`), which answers them as proposals in [its README](system/README.md#the-questions-of-the-brief-as-the-system-answers-them): the first (the page opens in full only by the person), the ninth (a third theme for bright sunlight, as a switch; whether it comes on by itself stays open), the tenth (a Place without a photograph is shown by its own plan), and the price of the tilted map (a saver mode is drawn; the measurement still needs a phone).

1. **How much does the Story screen carry?** It keeps the tilted map above and the guide's page below, with the player floating over the page: on a phone five lines of the Story show at a time. `OUT-2` asks that position, the next Place, distance and time be seen, and all four are. Is the balance right, or should the page open to full height by itself when a Story begins?
2. **On the web, "the Outing" is listening by hand.** The page says in words that Stories start by themselves and play without a network only in the application (`OUT-20`, specification §6). Is that the right thing to say to someone who opened a link?
3. **The verdict of the check is stated in words**, with the time of the check: "fits your three hours, 15 minutes to spare; every place is seen from outside". Is this the tone — and is "checked at" what `RTE-12` means to show?
4. **A real English name can be an address** ("Karla Marksa Street 5, Minsk"), and a street name in English can be a transliteration. The design shows the data as it is. Whether such names are good enough for a visitor is a question about the data, not about the drawing.
5. **Product words are set in sentence case in the interface** — «Начать прогулку», not «Начать Прогулку». The glossary's capitals are kept for documents.
6. **The time before the limit is shown as a reserve** ("15 minutes to spare"), which reads `RTE-5`'s buffer as something the person should see.
7. **A sentence of a Story is a control**: a tap shows its source or starts a Correction (SC-6). The mockups mark the source with a numeral; the tap itself is drawn in `D-03`.
8. **The Android phone looks plainer than the iPhone**, by the decision of round two. Is that acceptable to show to an investor on an Android phone, or should Android get a richer treatment of its own in `D-02`?
9. **Glass and the sun.** Should the design system carry a "bright sunlight" setting that makes every pane solid — and should it switch on by itself?
10. **A photograph is the head of the guide's page.** A Place without a free photograph will exist. Is a page without a photograph acceptable there, or does such a Place need a drawing of its own?
