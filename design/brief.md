## The owner's choice

**Not made yet.** The three directions are in [`directions/`](directions/) and open with `pnpm design` ([how](README.md)). The owner chooses one, or asks for another round; the answer is written here, with its date and the owner's remarks, and `D-01` is closed with it.

## 1. Who uses it, and where

**People.** A Party of one or more: a visitor with three hours before a train, friends deciding where to go on Saturday, a person from the city who wants to see it anew (scenarios SC-1…SC-3). Most are Guests at first: the product is usable without an account (`NFR-15`). Inside the company: a Curator, a Moderator and an Administrator at a desk, in the console.

**Conditions.** They decide what the design may ask of a person.

| Condition                                     | What follows for the design                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Requirement                 |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Walking, the phone in one hand or in a pocket | One glance has to answer "where next, how far, am I on time". Controls sit where a thumb reaches, at the bottom.                                                                                                                | `NFR-24`, `OUT-2`, `OUT-18` |
| Bright sun                                    | Contrast well above the minimum, heavier weights, no meaning carried by a pale tint alone. A light theme is the daytime default; dark is a choice.                                                                              | `NFR-24`                    |
| Headphones on, the screen locked              | Audio is the main channel: the screen is what a person looks at when they _choose_ to. Everything shown while a Story plays must also work as text for someone who cannot hear it.                                              | `NFR-25`, `NFR-27`, `OUT-5` |
| No network                                    | What needs a network is shown as unavailable, never hidden; the state of the Route Pack is one plain status.                                                                                                                    | `OUT-14…16`                 |
| A link opened from a messenger                | The Route Page and the Invitation must explain themselves on a phone's browser in the first screen, without an account.                                                                                                         | `EDT-7`, `PTY-3`            |
| Two languages                                 | Russian and English from the first screen. Real names are long in both; nothing may depend on a name being short.                                                                                                               | `I18N-*`                    |
| Three alphabets                               | Russian; Belarusian in names; and the Belarusian Latin alphabet, in which the map and Wikidata give many English names — Poliakaŭ, Abrampoĺski, Niezaliežnasci. A typeface that lacks ў or ŭ cannot set the product's own data. | `I18N-2`, ADR-0008          |
| A desk, a long session                        | The console is dense and quiet: tables, an editor, a map. One family with the product, never mistaken for it.                                                                                                                   | `CUR-*`                     |

**Numbers the design is held to.** Touch targets of at least 44 pt on iOS and 48 dp on Android, which also clears the 24 CSS px of WCAG 2.2 criterion 2.5.8; text contrast of at least 4.5 : 1 and 3 : 1 for large text and for graphics (criteria 1.4.3 and 1.4.11); text that can grow to 200 % (1.4.4); nothing that can be done only by dragging (2.5.7) — a sheet, a reorder and a scrubber each need a second way. Sources: [Apple, accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility), [Android, accessibility](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/accessibility/apps), [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/). No platform publishes a rule for sunlight; the position taken here — aim above the minimums, prefer weight, try it outdoors — is ours, and trying it outdoors is the owner's part in `D-05`.

## 2. What it should feel like

The owner's words: modern, fitted to the subject, intuitive, made by the best practice of each platform, unique — good enough for a front-end award, and with no thought that a machine made it.

Read against the product, that becomes five qualities:

1. **Trustworthy before it is pretty.** The product's promises are a Route that has been checked and facts that have sources (specification §1.3). The design shows the check and the source as part of the content, not as badges.
2. **Made for a glance.** One number, one name, one state at a time while walking; detail when the person stops.
3. **Of a city, not of an app store.** Its forms come from how cities have always spoken to people on foot — plans, timetables, bills — and not from other applications.
4. **Quiet about itself.** The voice and the street are the experience. The interface steps back while a Story plays.
5. **At home on each system.** Navigation, back, sheets, system controls and the lock screen follow the platform; the character is in type, colour, the map and the way content is set.

## 3. What it must never look like

Every screen is reviewed against this list, here and again in `D-05`.

| Never                                                                     | Why                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A stock gradient, a glow, a blurred blob of colour as background          | It is the look of a template, and it carries no meaning.                                                                                                                                                                     |
| Glass panels for their own sake                                           | On iOS the system's own bars may be glass, where Apple puts glass: the layer of navigation and controls. Our content never is. ([Apple, materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials)) |
| Pictographic characters used as icons                                     | Icons are drawn; a character from a keyboard is not an icon.                                                                                                                                                                 |
| An interchangeable grid of rounded cards with a photo, a title and a chip | It is the default look of the whole category (section 5), and it hides order — a Route is a sequence.                                                                                                                        |
| Filler copy, invented names, invented history                             | Every name, time and distance in a mockup is real; Story text is quoted and cited.                                                                                                                                           |
| A chat with an assistant as the main surface                              | The Route is decided by a planner and checked by a verifier (`RTE-3`, `RTE-6`), not negotiated in a transcript; and free text is hard to use on a walk.                                                                      |
| A mascot, confetti, streaks, awards                                       | The specification removed leaderboards and awards (§1.4).                                                                                                                                                                    |
| A map dressed as another company's map                                    | The map is ours to style (ADR-0008); a default-looking map says "a wrapper around someone else's product".                                                                                                                   |
| Colour as the only carrier of a state                                     | A verdict is always said in words as well.                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Tiny grey text on white for anything that matters outdoors                | See the conditions above.                                                                                                                                                                                                    |

**The look of the category.** Across these pages the same interface repeats: a map with pins as the home screen, a mini-player at the bottom, cards with a photo and a title, a download step, permission screens. Nothing found describes a typographic or cartographic identity of its own. A product that looks like that is taken for one more of them; all three directions below leave that look on purpose.

**What people complain about**, in the reviews quoted on those listings: a Story that starts late, twice or never; an offline state that is unclear or breaks; not finding oneself on the map; friction with accounts. The first three are answered by requirements (`OUT-3…4`, `OUT-14…15`, `OUT-2`) and each has to be _visible_ in the design: why a Story did not start, what exactly is on the device, where one is.

**How each was tested against the bar.** Each direction was looked at beside the list of section 3 and beside the category's default look of section 5. A fourth idea was dropped at this step: an "instrument" with a dark panel, a dial and an amber light — it would have been taken for a familiar template of the day, and it made a planner look like a gadget.

**Sofia Sans was changed.** The typeface has no ŭ and no Ŭ, which the Belarusian Latin alphabet needs. Both were composed from the typeface's own u and its own breve by [`tools/add-ubreve.py`](tools/add-ubreve.py); the rule and the result are recorded in `MODIFIED.txt` beside the files. The Open Font License allows a modified version, and the typeface reserves no name. If direction 2 is chosen, `D-02` decides between keeping this change, asking the authors to take it, and another typeface.

The font files are the subsets for Latin, extended Latin and Cyrillic published as the Fontsource packages of version 5.3.0; the Open Font License allows subsetting and redistribution with the licence text. ## 9. What the mockups ask the owner

Each of these is a place where a drawing had to decide how to read the specification. A "no" to any of them is as useful as the choice of a direction.

1. **Does the screen of a Story being played need the map?** Direction 1 keeps a band of it at the top; directions 2 and 3 put it one key away and use the room for the text. `OUT-2` asks that position, the next Place, distance and time be seen; all three show the next Place, distance and time, and only the first shows position.
2. **On the web, "the Outing" is listening by hand.** The page says in words that Stories start by themselves and play without a network only in the application (`OUT-20`, specification §6). Is that the right thing to say to someone who opened a link?
3. **The verdict of the check is stated in a sentence**, with the time of the check: "fits your three hours, back at 16:45, 15 minutes to spare; every place is seen from outside". Is this the tone — and is "checked at" what `RTE-12` means to show?
4. **A real English name can be an address** ("Karla Marksa Street 5, Minsk"), and a street name in English can be a transliteration. The design shows the data as it is. Whether such names are good enough for a visitor is a question about the data, not about the drawing.
5. **Product words are set in sentence case in the interface** — «Начать прогулку», not «Начать Прогулку». The glossary's capitals are kept for documents.
6. **The time before the limit is shown as a reserve** ("15 minutes to spare"), which reads `RTE-5`'s buffer as something the person should see.
7. **A sentence of a Story is a control**: a tap shows its source or starts a Correction (SC-6). The mockups mark the source with a numeral; the tap itself is drawn in `D-03`.
